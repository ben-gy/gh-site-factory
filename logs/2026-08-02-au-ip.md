## Architecture Decisions

**The load-bearing decision was how to read the source at all.** IP RAPID ships as a
single 1.33 GB ZIP that expands to **9.51 GB** across six CSVs, with no per-table
resource URL. Downloading and extracting it on a CI runner is not viable.

`pipeline/zipstream.mjs` reads the **Zip64 central directory over HTTP range
requests**, then range-fetches and `zlib.createInflateRaw`s only the three members
that matter, streaming line-by-line in constant memory:

| Member | Compressed | Expanded | Used for |
|---|---|---|---|
| `application.csv` | 82 MB | 720 MB | filing year, status, sub-type, lifecycle dates |
| `party_activity.csv` | 358 MB | 1.79 GB | founding applicants, agents of record |
| `application_classification.csv` | 244 MB | 2.07 GB | WIPO-35 fields, Nice classes |
| `application_events.csv` | 483 MB | 4.21 GB | **never touched** |

Full run: **~95 seconds**, ~684 MB transferred, nothing written to disk. Member
offsets are re-read every run because they move on each weekly rebuild, and deflate
streams are not seekable so each member is inflated from its own data start.

Between passes an `appMap` carries the join key, packing three facts into one integer
per application (filing year, origin rank, country-counted flag) — a 4.3M-entry Map of
objects is the difference between running on a CI runner and not.

**Vanilla TS over React:** nine hash-routed views with no shared component tree.
**No chart library:** every view needed custom hover targets and semantic colour,
which is most of what a chart library would have supplied.

## Errors & Resolutions

### 1. Silent loss of every plant breeder's right (caught by inspection, not by CI)
I guessed the `ip_right_type` value was `plant_breeders_right`. It is **`pbr`**. All
11,649 records were dropped — and the run stayed green, because gate 1 reconciled
`appMap.size + noYear` against rows *it had already accepted*, which is a tautology.
Fixed by anchoring the gate to the **source row count** (4,332,386) and making an
unrecognised `ip_right_type` a hard failure. This is the second time in this fleet a
tautological gate has shipped a silent deletion.

### 2. Pre-1990 "origin" is not missing at random
Charting domestic vs foreign from 1980 produced "Australian +715%" — spectacular and
entirely false. 87% of 1980 patents have **no recorded applicant country**; the growth
was the field being back-filled. The pipeline now computes, per right type, the first
year from which the unrecorded share stays under 30%, and each divergence panel starts
there (patents 1988, trade marks 1958, designs 1981, PBR 1989). Designs never fall
below ~11%, which is stated on the panel rather than hidden.

### 3. Two colour-encoding defects that passed CI and looked plausible
The technology heatmap and the state choropleth both used the log ramp. Log is correct
for the world map (a thousandfold range across countries) and wrong for both of those
(2–5× within a row; ~3.2× across eight states) — it pushed every cell into the top band
and painted a uniform block. Both now use a linear ramp; the world map keeps log.

### 4. Adversarial review: 19 confirmed defects, all fixed
35 candidates, each verified by an independent agent told to refute it. **16 refuted,
19 confirmed.** The critical one:

> The attorney client map **published a company that does not exist.** `buildAttorneys`
> formed a client edge from every applicant whose filer key was blank, so all 538,824
> pseudonymised applicants collapsed into one client that took whichever raw name it
> saw first — "Ооо Бостонджин" — and was rendered as the **largest client of 37 of 60
> firms**, on the same page that stated those records were excluded.

Fixed: blank keys never form an edge; the volume is counted per firm and disclosed as
"applicant not nameable". Spruson Ferguson's top clients are now Kimberly-Clark, Canon,
Shell and Johnson & Johnson.

Also confirmed and fixed: survival curves counted **right-censored** rights as
survivors (the 2010s cohort flatlined near 100%; now uses an at-risk denominator and
stops where observation does, at 9 years / 42.7%); country totals counted applicant
**rows** while labelled "applications"; sorting any column **silently killed every
row's click handler**; sort controls were unreachable by keyboard; `zipstream` decoded
each inflate chunk independently, corrupting multi-byte characters on chunk boundaries;
chart bounds were pinned to a literal 2026; the part-year band was exactly zero pixels
wide; specialisation was rounded twice.

**A correction to my own reporting:** I initially described the Unicode fix as rescuing
a large population of non-Latin names. Measured against the source, only **1,826** of
4.5M founding-applicant names contain non-ASCII characters and the old normaliser
produced just 12 empty keys — the phantom client was almost entirely the redacted
tokens. The Unicode fix is correct but small (284 keys, mostly Danish and Polish).

### 5. A claim from the research phase that did not reproduce
The research agent reported Aristocrat (12,573) out-filing CSIRO (9,909) as Australia's
most prolific filer. It does not reproduce: that count includes every `party_activity`
row, which is the double-counting trap the same agent had warned about. Counting
**founding applicants only**, CSIRO leads with 7,591 against Aristocrat's 6,896. The
site ships the correct figure and the claim was not published.

### 6. Verification-harness confusion (not a site defect)
Several early "dead click" results were my own coordinate-space error — the Chrome tool
takes screenshot coordinates (1456 wide) while `getBoundingClientRect` returns CSS
pixels (1728 wide). Once corrected, every interactive element responded to real clicks.
Worth recording because a synthetic `dispatchEvent` *passed* the whole time, which is
precisely the false-pass the patterns warn about.
