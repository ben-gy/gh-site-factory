## Why it is not deployed

Both findings were reproduced by me directly, not taken from an agent's report.

**1. `robots.txt` disallows the acquisition path.** Fetched verbatim from
`https://www.grants.gov.au/robots.txt`:

```
User-agent: *
Disallow: /Search/*
Disallow: /Reports/*
Disallow: /admin*
```

`/Reports/*` is where `GaPublishedShow` and `GaPublishedDownload` live. There is
no alternative route — no API, no data.gov.au bulk distribution. The only
bulk-acquisition path is the one path excluded from crawling, and ToU cl. 1.1.1
binds users to "instructions … as advised on GrantConnect".

**2. The Terms of Use grant an open licence and restrict reuse in the same
section, with no precedence clause.**

- cl. 5.1: *"information published on GrantConnect is licensed under a Creative
  Commons Attribution 3.0 Australia licence (CC BY 3.0 AU)"* — the grant is real.
- cl. 5.5(a)(ii): *"You must not: … frame or reformat the files, pages, images,
  information and materials from this website on any other website"* — a literal
  description of this build. Headed "Linking to this Site", which is an argument
  it is confined to framing; cl. 0(p) says headings "may be used for
  interpreting" the text, not that they limit it.
- cl. 5.4: *"Users are not permitted to use the GO materials to … increase
  traffic to your website"* — a restriction CC BY 3.0 AU does not contain.

Publishing is outward-facing and hard to undo. An unresolved licence is not a
licence, so the run stopped short of publishing and left the call to a person.
ToU cl. 5.3 nominates `grantconnect@finance.gov.au` for exactly this question;
the three questions that need a yes are listed in DEPLOYMENT-HOLD.md.

The collector was stopped as soon as this was found. It had completed one full
historical pull at ~1 req/sec with a browser UA; no rate limiting or block was
ever encountered.

## What was verified before the block

**The 50,001-row silent truncation — reproduced exactly.**

```
GET /Reports/GaPublishedDownload?AgencyStatus=-1&DateType=Publish%20Date
      &DateStart=01-Jan-2025&DateEnd=31-Dec-2025
  -> HTTP 200, 8,995,995 bytes, valid XLSX
     in-file "Count" cell : 93,667
     actual data rows     : 50,001
     row date range       : 2025-04-03 → 2025-07-10  (NOT the requested year)
```

No error, no warning, no partial-content status. Because the rows are not in date
order, the missing 46.6% cannot be inferred from the range of what arrived.

**The gate is free and self-contained.** The workbook's criteria preamble carries
a `Count` cell the server computes independently of the rows it writes. The
collector compares parsed rows to that cell for every slice and bisects the date
range on mismatch. A full pull reconciled to **359,829 awards / $235,127,835,925**
— exactly the count the register reports for the same criteria — with **zero
truncations at monthly granularity** (largest month: April 2025, 20,352 awards).

**Independent confirmation.** A separate agent, working only from the endpoint
list, derived the same recipe and added a second free anchor: the summed
`Value (AUD)` reconciles to the workbook's reported total to the cent
(Jan 2025: 2,035,484,665.60 both ways).

## Data traps found and handled

1. **Sparse XLSX cells.** Empty cells are omitted, not emitted as blanks. Reading
   in document order shifts every later value one column left — producing a
   plausible table with states in the postcode column and no error anywhere.
   Cells are placed by decoding their `r` reference (`AB24` → column 27).
2. **Dates are Excel serials**, not strings (`45664.5913657407` = 2025-01-07
   14:11:34), under numFmt `d-mmm-yyyy`.
3. **`Delivery State/Territory` is multi-valued** — the real data contains
   `"VIC, Overseas"`, `"NSW, Overseas"`, `"National"`, `"Overseas"` and blanks.
   A naive group-by invents a `"VIC, Overseas"` state; a naive choropleth drops
   those awards silently. The map is built only from awards naming exactly one
   Australian jurisdiction, and states its own coverage gap on the view.
4. **Aggregate rows carry the literal recipient name `"Aggregate"`** and ABN
   `"Aggregate"`. Left in a recipient leaderboard they merge into one fake
   mega-recipient outranking every real organisation. Excluded from every
   recipient-level view and given a view of their own.
5. **`ABN Exempt` / `n/a` are not identifiers.** Using them as a grouping key
   collapses every exempt recipient in the country into one entity.
6. **Selection-process vocabulary is inconsistently cased** — `Closed
   Non-Competitive` alongside `Open Non-competitive`. Normalised case-insensitively.
7. **Demand-driven is not a choice.** It is the large majority of records by
   count (childcare subsidy, apprenticeship incentives). Folding it into
   "non-competitive" yields the true-sounding, false headline "3% of Commonwealth
   grants are competitively awarded". Every competitive share on the site is
   computed over *discretionary* awards only, and an agency with no discretionary
   awards reports **no share rather than 0%** — a rate over an empty denominator
   is exactly how a fabricated finding gets manufactured from real numbers.

## Privacy — a second constraint, fixed in code

An independent audit sampled the register three ways and found **~5.9% of records
(order of 21,000 of 359,829) name a natural person**, not an organisation, paired
with a locality — in sampled cases a town of ~200 people and a postcode of ~570.

My first allowlist was wrong: it kept Recipient Name, ABN and postcode
unconditionally, which would have shipped a searchable directory of private
citizens who received small government payments.

`pipeline/privacy.mjs` now classifies every recipient and withholds name, ABN,
recipient postcode and delivery postcode for anything not positively identifiable
as an organisation. It is an **allowlist on names**, not a denylist. Critically,
**an ABN is not treated as evidence of being an organisation** — sole traders hold
ABNs, so classifying on ABN presence would let through precisely the people the
rule protects. Redaction runs at ingest *and* again at aggregation, so a slice
file written before the rule existed cannot leak one.

A build-failing gate rejects any future recipient-side field matching
`/contact|email|phone|address|suburb|dob|…/` that is not on a reviewed list.
`tests/privacy.test.ts` tests it by injecting `Recipient Email Address`.

## Architecture decisions

- **Vanilla TS, no framework.** One hash router, no nested component tree.
- **Zero-dependency pipeline, including a hand-rolled XLSX reader** (`pipeline/xlsx.mjs`,
  ZIP via `node:zlib` + SpreadsheetML). A spreadsheet library is a large amount of
  attack surface for "unzip and read some XML" in an unattended CI job.
- **NDJSON slices with resume.** The first full run died silently writing a ~430 MB
  `JSON.stringify` — the fetch loop's success line was already in the log and the
  process simply never wrote the file. Now each month streams to its own NDJSON file
  and a re-run skips months already on disk. The three most recent months always
  refetch, because agencies revise awards.
- **Per-financial-year chunks, dictionary-encoded.** The register as one array is a
  ~90 MB download. Nothing is sampled or capped — it is chunked and loaded on demand,
  with the size shown to the reader on the "load every year" control.

## Errors & Resolutions

| Problem | Resolution |
|---|---|
| `GaPublishedShow` 404 on first attempt | Not a session problem — the criteria are stateless query params on the 302's `Location`. Skip the POST entirely and GET `GaPublishedShow`/`GaPublishedDownload` directly. |
| Bulk export silently truncates at 50,001 rows | Monthly slicing + reconcile every file against its own in-workbook `Count`; bisect and refetch on mismatch; throw on an irreducible single-day mismatch. |
