## Idea Source

Researched. `IDEAS.md` was empty, so a six-lane research workflow (live/real-time,
consumer money, health & safety, IP & legal, environment & nature, infra & telecom)
produced 18 candidates, each required to prove its primary source with a real `curl`
rather than assert it. A three-judge panel then scored every candidate on data
integrity, product value and visual richness.

The panel split three ways — data-integrity picked `au-power-plans`, product-value
picked `au-bulkbilling`, visual-richness picked `au-ip`. Ranked by mean, the top four
were within 1.7 points of each other:

| | mean | integrity | value | richness |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| au-advisers | 78.7 | 83 | 68 | 85 |
| au-bulkbilling | 78.3 | 82 | 91 | 62 |
| **au-towers** | **78.0** | **88** | **80** | **66** |
| au-fuel | 77.0 | 85 | 74 | 72 |

Picked **au-towers** over the marginal mean-leader on three grounds: it had the
highest data-integrity score in the field (exact lat/lon on every row, needing no
geocoding, plus eight years of depth); it carries no personal-data risk, where
`au-advisers` is a searchable index of ~15,000 named individuals with disciplinary
history that would go stale between monthly pipeline runs; and the richness knock
against it was "shape adjacency to au-branches", which is a design problem I could
solve rather than a data limitation. `au-bulkbilling` had the best product value on
the board but was explicitly criticised for a view stack identical to au-welfare,
au-immunisation, au-jobs and au-income.

`au-fuel` was passed over because good incumbents already exist (fuelaustralia.org
is one of this factory's own quality benchmarks).

## Architecture Decisions

**The organising idea.** A mobile network is not one thing. A tower's *reach* is set
by the frequencies it transmits on, not by its generation label: sub-1 GHz carries
tens of kilometres across country, 1.8–3.6 GHz does not, so a "5G tower" running
only NR3600 reaches nobody new. Every view is built on frequency rather than on
"has 5G", and that single decision is what makes the site say something carrier
coverage maps cannot.

**Designed away from the house stack.** A three-designer workflow (consumer,
accountability, technology angles) plus a director synthesis produced a set with
exactly **one** choropleth, **zero** ranked-bar charts and **no** treemap. The map's
fill is a categorical carrier-*set* encoding, so it answers "do I get a choice here"
rather than "how many towers are here" — which is also what keeps it from being
au-branches with different dots.

**Two encodings were reversed after looking at the real data**, not after a review:

1. The co-funded distribution was designed and built as a per-tower beeswarm. With
   2,161 towers piled around the mode, the collision-relaxation had to shrink dots
   to a 0.35 px hairline to stay non-overlapping, and it rendered as a smear. Replaced
   with a histogram whose bins are clickable, so per-tower detail is one click away.
   The beeswarm code and its tests were deleted rather than left dead.
2. A "the money went to well-covered areas by 106×" statistic was computed and
   *rejected before shipping*: the thinnest-covered quartile of regional Australia
   *is* the largest land, so any per-1,000-km² rate there mostly measures its size.
   Replaced with the scale-free co-funded **share** of towers, which reverses the
   conclusion to the honest one — 23.6% in the thinnest quarter against 6.2% in the
   best-covered, i.e. the program broadly worked.

**Definitional choice, stated on the site.** An SA3 is large enough that nearly every
one contains all three carriers somewhere, so a bare presence test says "everywhere is
competitive" and is useless. A carrier counts as a *real option* where its long-reach
site count reaches one third of the best-served carrier's. Raw per-carrier counts are
always on the hover so a reader can disagree.

## Source-file traps handled

All four found by inspection of the real files, all covered by tests:

- `mobile-sites-optus-2023.csv` is **cp1252**, not UTF-8 — decoding the set as UTF-8 throws.
- `mobile-sites-telstra-2021-v2.csv` carries **20 rows with Year values 2022–2041**.
  An early probe that did not filter these attributed a phantom 3G site to Telstra 2025.
- The **2018 and 2019 files have no site identifier**, so per-tower history can only
  start in 2020. Keying a panel across that boundary invents ~23,000 "new" sites in 2020.
- **~3,100 rows across all years share a registered site number** with another row —
  separate installations at one site — and are merged by OR-ing their band flags.

Plus two found during the build: 617 coastal sites fell outside the generalised
polygons and are snapped to the nearest boundary within 20 km (1 genuinely unplaced,
on an external territory); and 19 ABS pseudo-regions have null geometry.

## Pre-deploy review

A 40-agent adversarial review ran across four lenses (data accuracy, misleading
presentation, code correctness, accessibility & mobile), with every finding
independently refuted-or-confirmed by a separate agent. **36 raised, 10 refuted,
26 confirmed — all fixed** in `4366a92` before hand-over.

The two that mattered:

1. **Every reconciliation gate was a tautology.** A reviewer copied the pipeline,
   narrowed the coordinate filter, silently deleted 601 real towers across Tasmania
   and southern Victoria — and all six gates still printed green, while the README
   promised they would stop exactly that. They compared derived objects to each
   other; none was anchored to anything the pipeline had not itself constructed.
   Replaced with a source-anchored gate (accepted + rejected rows must equal each
   file's own row count, and only the known bad-Year rows may be rejected), then
   **verified it fails on that exact injected fault**.
2. **Canberra was classified "Rest of state"** because the ACT's ABS GCCSA is
   "Australian Capital Territory", not "Greater Canberra". The Targeting view told
   readers twice that capital cities were excluded while including all ten Canberra
   SA3s, which dragged the best-covered quartile's figure down. Fixed; that figure
   moved 4.8% → 6.2%.

Also confirmed and fixed: the histogram's terminal bar was labelled "93–120 km" but
held towers to 317 km; the Region view drew band *presence* as a full-width bar and
then invited comparison against a national-*share* diamond (two scales on one lane);
My Address measured a region's tower centroid without saying so; co-funded counts were
re-derived in the browser from a rounded distance and printed 453/2,161 where the front
page said 455/2,165; SVG controls announced as buttons did nothing on Enter/Space;
four colours failed WCAG AA; both dialogs claimed `aria-modal` without moving, trapping
or restoring focus and leaked a keydown listener per non-Escape close; no `aria-current`
or `aria-pressed` anywhere; tooltips were mouse-only; tap targets and iOS zoom-on-focus.
`attachSvgZoom` was also hardened — it threw where SVG geometry is unavailable, taking
the whole view down.

## Per-view UX critique

| View | Question it answers | Hover | Click | Why not the default |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Overview | What is going on nationally? | exact counts per year and series | a finding card → its view | rule-generated findings, each showing its rule |
| Coverage | Do I get a choice of network here? | per-carrier long-reach vs total, with the verdict per carrier | region → Region verdict; legend → isolate that category; tower → site detail | colour encodes *who*, not *how many* |
| My Address | What reaches this exact spot? | site, distance, bands, funding | marker → site detail | point-level, because a region is too coarse to answer it |
| Spectrum | Reach, or speed? | band, MHz, count, share, national share | scope and year controls | axis is a physical quantity, not a ranking |
| Switch-Off | What happened to my tower? | the exact transition and its size | ribbon → cohort with figures | tracks entity identity, not totals |
| Public Money | Did the money fill a gap? | bin range, count, per-carrier split | bar → the towers inside it | a computed relationship between records |
| Capture | Did each carrier win its share? | both shares, both counts, the ratio | panel → Targeting for that state | the finding is a ratio near 1, not a tallest bar |
| Targeting | Did the money follow need? | region, area, towers then and now, share funded | dot or row → Region verdict | needs a pre-program baseline on one axis |
| Regions | So which one should I be on? | per-band local vs national share | chips and jump buttons → other views | reuses the frequency axis, so a gap reads as a hole at a frequency |

## Errors & Resolutions

- **mapshaper ESM default export** — `applyCommands is not a function`; needed `.default`.
- **19 ABS pseudo-regions with null geometry** crashed the point-in-polygon index; filtered.
- **Over-simplification gate was wrong** — it failed on genuinely simple regions (ABS ships
  "Unley" with 7 vertices). Re-scoped to the real failure mode: a *large* region flattened
  into a box.
- **The map fitted its layer bounds**, which include Christmas and Norfolk Islands, shrinking
  Australia to a smudge. Now opens on the mainland.
- **The map's multi-carrier greys were greenish** and competed with the carrier colours, so
  the eye went to the well-served regions instead of the ones with no choice. Neutralised.
- **Zone labels collided** with the longest band names on the ladder; rotated into the gutter.
- **The preview server served a stale bundle** through the whole first verification pass —
  caught by comparing the live bundle hash against the local `dist`, which is why that check
  is worth running rather than trusting a screenshot.
